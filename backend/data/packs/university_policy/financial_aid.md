## 1. Introduction and Philosophy of Student Aid

Western Oak University is committed to fostering socioeconomic diversity and academic distinction by ensuring educational access for qualified students. Financial aid packages comprise merit scholarships, need-based tuition grants, institutional fellowships, federal and private work-study allocations, and educational loan facilities. 

Every recipient of institutional aid assumes contractual obligations regarding academic progress, minimum enrollment intensities, behavioral good standing, and prompt notification of changes in enrollment status.

---

## 2. Institutional Merit Scholarships and Academic Endowments

### 2.1 Undergraduate Merit Programs
Institutional scholarships are awarded by the Admissions Scholarship Committee upon initial admission:
- **Presidential Excellence Scholarship:** Awards $12,000 annually ($6,000 per regular semester) to entering first-year students demonstrating exceptional academic achievement.
- **Dean's Leadership Grant:** Awards $7,500 annually ($3,750 per regular semester) recognizing academic rigor, civic service, and extracurricular commitment.
- **Provost's STEM Achievement Award:** Awards $5,000 annually to students pursuing accredited programs in engineering, mathematics, computer science, or physical sciences.

### 2.2 Scholarship Renewal Criteria and Cumulative GPA Minimums
To maintain and renew an institutional scholarship for subsequent academic years, recipients must satisfy the following strict criteria evaluated at the conclusion of each Spring semester:
1. **Enrollment Intensity:** Maintain continuous full-time undergraduate enrollment of at least 12 credit hours per regular semester.
2. **Cumulative Academic Standing:**
   - Presidential Excellence: Minimum cumulative GPA of 3.40.
   - Dean's Leadership Grant: Minimum cumulative GPA of 3.20.
   - Provost's STEM Award: Minimum cumulative GPA of 3.00.
3. **Credit Accumulation:** Successfully earn at least 24 new credit hours per academic year.

Students who fall below these renewal standards forfeit their scholarship funding for the subsequent academic cycle without retroactive recovery.

## 3. Need-Based University Tuition Grants

Western Oak Need-Based Grants are calculated utilizing institutional methodology based on the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) and the College Scholarship Service (CSS) Profile. Need-based grant amounts vary between $1,500 and $10,000 per academic year, capped at the recipient's demonstrated financial need. 

Recipients must maintain Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) under Section 6 of the Academic Policy Handbook (minimum 2.00 cumulative GPA and 67% course completion pace).

---

## 4. Student Employment and Federal Work-Study Regulations

Work-study programs provide part-time employment opportunities for matriculated students with financial need:
- Students may work a maximum of 20 hours per week during regular instructional periods.
- During official university break intervals (winter recess, spring break), students may work up to 35 hours per week subject to departmental budgetary clearance.
- Earnings are disbursed bi-weekly directly to the student employee and are not automatically applied toward bursar account balances unless an explicit direct deposit deduction agreement is executed.

## 5. Course Drop, Withdrawal, and Tuition Refund Intersections

### 5.1 Financial Aid Adjustments on Enrollment Changes
When a financial aid recipient reduces their enrolled credit hours or initiates a formal withdrawal from the university, federal and institutional regulations mandate a recalculation of tuition liabilities and awarded scholarship or grant funds.

Financial aid awards are originally calculated based upon full-time enrollment assumptions. If a student drops below full-time status (12 credits) before the official University Census Date (end of Week 1), institutional scholarships are canceled in full, leaving the student directly liable for outstanding balances.

### 5.2 Mandatory Withdrawal Refund Schedule for Financial Aid Recipients
In order to comply with institutional financial aid liability frameworks, university tuition refunds following an official term withdrawal are governed by the Financial Aid Institutional Adjustment Schedule. 

When a student who is the recipient of any institutional scholarship, university grant, or institutional tuition waiver completely withdraws from the university, the tuition refund is calibrated by instructional timeframe:

| Withdrawal Period | Week of Instruction | Applicable Tuition Refund |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Initial Enrollment Period | Week 1 of Semester | 100% Refund |
| Early Adjustment Period | Week 2 of Semester | 75% Refund |
| Primary Assessment Period | Week 3 of Semester | 50% Refund |
| Mid-Semester Period | Week 4 of Semester | 25% Refund |
| Final Instructional Period | Week 5 onwards | 0% Refund (No Refund) |

### 5.3 Specific Policy on Week-4 Term Withdrawals
As established in Section 5.2 and the refund schedule above, any student who officially withdraws from a course or from the university during the fourth week of the semester (instructional days 22 through 28) is entitled to an institutional tuition refund of exactly 25%. 

Institutional grant monies will be adjusted proportionately, and the remaining 75% of billed tuition remains a permanent financial obligation of the student. This policy ensures standardized accounting across all university bursar operations and state compliance standards.

## 6. Financial Aid Appeals, Deadlines, and Special Circumstances

### 6.1 Priority Application Deadlines
- **Annual FAFSA and CSS Submission Deadline:** February 15 prior to the subsequent academic year.
- **Scholarship Acceptance Confirmation Deadline:** May 1 for matriculating freshmen; June 15 for continuing students.
- **Satisfactory Academic Progress Appeal Deadline:** July 15 for the Autumn semester; December 15 for the Spring semester.

### 6.2 Professional Judgment and Special Circumstances Review
Students experiencing substantial unforeseen financial hardship—such as parental job termination, catastrophic medical expenditures, or death of a primary wage earner—may submit a Special Circumstances Appeal to the Financial Aid Review Committee. Appeals must be accompanied by third-party documentation, federal tax returns, and an itemized hardship statement.
