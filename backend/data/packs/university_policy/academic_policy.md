## 1. Institutional Overview and Governance

Western Oak University maintains rigorous standards of academic performance, intellectual integrity, and systematic evaluation. This handbook codifies the rights, responsibilities, thresholds, and institutional procedures governing undergraduate and graduate candidates. Every student registered in a degree, diploma, or certificate program is subject to the provisions herein. The Senate Academic Regulations Committee reviews these policies quadrennially, reserving authority to issue administrative rulings through the Office of the Registrar.

---

## 2. Degree Requirements and Credit Frameworks

### 2.1 Undergraduate Baccalaureate Requirements
To qualify for conferring of a baccalaureate degree, an undergraduate candidate must successfully complete a minimum of 120 semester credit hours. At least 45 credits must be completed in upper-division coursework designated at the 3000-level or higher. A minimum of 30 credit hours must be earned through direct residential instruction at Western Oak University, establishing institutional residency.

### 2.2 Normal Semester Credit Loads
A standard full-time undergraduate course schedule consists of 15 semester credit hours per regular academic semester (Autumn or Spring). The minimum registration threshold required to preserve full-time matriculation status, institutional housing eligibility, and visa verification is 12 semester credits.

The maximum normal credit load permissible for any undergraduate student during an Autumn or Spring term is 18 credits. Any course enrollment exceeding this ceiling requires formal approval via an Academic Overload Petition submitted to the Department Chair and Dean of Faculty.

### 2.3 Graduate Degree Credit Requirements
Candidates matriculating into Master of Science or Master of Arts programs must fulfill between 30 and 36 graduate credits at the 5000-level or higher, maintaining cumulative grades of B (3.00 GPA) or higher across all approved coursework. No undergraduate courses below the 4000-level may count toward graduate completion requirements.

## 4. Attendance Obligations and Medical Exceptions

### 4.1 General Classroom Attendance Standard
Active instructional engagement is vital for cumulative mastery. Undergraduate and graduate students must maintain a minimum physical classroom attendance rate of 75% across all scheduled lecture, laboratory, seminar, and discussion sessions in every enrolled course.

Instructors record class registers at the beginning of each scheduled period. If a student's unexcused absences exceed 25% of total class meetings, the instructor is authorized to issue an administrative failure grade of 'F-Att' (Failure Due to Non-Attendance), regardless of accumulated marks on examinations or assignments.

### 4.2 Approved Medical Exemption Provision
A student who experiences acute physical illness, severe surgical convalescence, or documented psychiatric trauma may request an Approved Medical Exemption. 

To qualify for an authorized attendance reduction, the student must submit comprehensive clinical documentation from a licensed healthcare specialist to the University Student Health Services Center within 5 business days of the initial absence. 

Upon official validation and written authorization from the Dean of Student Welfare, the minimum physical attendance threshold for the affected student is reduced to 60% for the specific term. This provision operates strictly as an institutional accommodation for documented medical necessity, preserving credit eligibility while safeguarding student convalescence.

## 5. Formal Grade Appeals Policy

### 5.1 Grounds for Appeal
Course instructors possess autonomous academic discretion in evaluating student assessments, assignments, lab work, and exams. Consequently, a grade appeal may not challenge an instructor's subjective pedagogical assessment. Valid grounds for appeal are restricted strictly to:
1. Mathematical calculation error in tabulating composite points.
2. Gross procedural deviation from criteria published in the official syllabus distributed during the first week.
3. Arbitrary and capricious evaluation applied unequally across enrolled students.

### 5.2 Mandatory Resolution Timelines and Protocol
The grade appeal procedure operates across sequential institutional tiers:

1. **Informal Conference:** The student must first arrange a direct consultation with the course instructor within 10 calendar days of official grade publication to seek informal reconciliation.
2. **Formal Written Petition:** If the matter is not resolved during informal conference, the student must submit a formal written Grade Appeal Petition to the Department Chair. The absolute deadline to lodge a formal grade appeal is exactly 30 calendar days following the official posting of the final semester grade report by the University Registrar. Appeals received after the expiration of 30 calendar days will be summarily dismissed without administrative review.
3. **Appellate Board Hearing:** The Department Chair convenes a three-member Faculty Review Panel within 14 calendar days of receiving a compliant petition. The panel conducts an evidentiary review, interviews both parties, and issues a final, binding determination within 7 calendar days of hearing conclusion.

## 6. Academic Standing, Probation, and Disqualification

### 6.1 Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP)
Undergraduate students must maintain both a semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) and cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of at least 2.00 on the 4.00 scale to retain Good Academic Standing. Graduate candidates must maintain a minimum CGPA of 3.00.

### 6.2 Academic Warning
Any undergraduate student whose semester GPA drops below 2.00 while maintaining a cumulative GPA of 2.00 or higher receives an official Letter of Academic Warning from the Registrar. An academic warning does not appear on official academic transcripts but requires an advising conference before subsequent term registration.

### 6.3 Academic Probation Mandates
A student whose cumulative GPA drops below 2.00 at the end of any regular academic semester is immediately placed on Academic Probation. While on academic probation:
1. The student may not register for more than 13 semester credit hours in the subsequent term.
2. The student is prohibited from holding executive office in student governance organizations, athletic teams, or recognized campus fraternities and sororities.
3. The student must formulate and sign an Academic Recovery Contract with their designated academic advisor, mandating weekly tutoring sessions at the Student Academic Support Center.

### 6.4 Academic Suspension and Dismissal
A student placed on academic probation who fails to raise their cumulative GPA to 2.00 or achieve a term GPA of 2.25 after two consecutive semesters on probation is subject to Formal Academic Suspension for a duration of one calendar year (two full semesters). 

Following suspension, readmission requires submission of an Appeal for Academic Reinstatement to the Academic Standing Committee, demonstrating substantive remediation or external academic productivity. A second failure following reinstatement results in permanent Academic Dismissal without possibility of readmission.

## 7. Examination Protocols and Academic Integrity

### 7.1 Midterm and Final Examinations
Official final examinations must occur exclusively during the designated Final Examination Period designated in the University Calendar. No faculty member may reschedule, pre-date, or waive a final examination without express prior authorization from the Dean of the College. Students having three or more examinations scheduled within a single 24-hour block have the right to request rescheduling of the middle examination by notifying the instructor at least 14 days prior to the start of examination week.

### 7.2 Examination Hall Protocols
Candidates must display their official Western Oak University Photo Identification Card on the examination desk throughout the evaluation. All electronic devices, cellular telephones, smart watches, and unauthorized paper materials must be powered off and deposited in designated perimeter storage racks. 

Tardiness exceeding 30 minutes past the commencement of an examination constitutes automatic forfeiture, resulting in a recorded grade of zero. No student may depart the examination venue during the first 45 minutes or the final 15 minutes of the allocated testing window.

### 7.3 Course Incompletes
A grade of 'I' (Incomplete) may be granted exclusively when extenuating documented circumstances (such as severe trauma, hospitalization, or military deployment) prevent a student who is currently passing the course with a grade of C or higher from completing minor outstanding requirements (not exceeding 20% of total course work). 

The incomplete coursework must be submitted to the instructor within 60 calendar days following the conclusion of the semester. If the instructor does not submit a grade change report within this timeframe, the Incomplete grade permanently converts to an 'F'.

## 8. Graduation Clearances and Honors

### 8.1 Commencement and Degree Conferral
Degrees are conferred thrice annually: May, August, and December. Students must submit an Application for Degree Candidacy through the Registrar portal by the published deadline in the academic calendar (typically the fourth week of their final semester). 

Candidates must settle all outstanding university indebtedness, return all library holdings, and obtain clearance signatures from Financial Services, the Library, and the Academic Advising Office before diplomas and official transcripts are released.

### 8.2 Latin Honors Criteria
Graduating seniors who have completed at least 60 residential credits at Western Oak University are eligible for Latin Honors based upon final cumulative GPA:
- *Cum Laude:* 3.500 – 3.699 CGPA
- *Magna Cum Laude:* 3.700 – 3.899 CGPA
- *Summa Cum Laude:* 3.900 – 4.000 CGPA
