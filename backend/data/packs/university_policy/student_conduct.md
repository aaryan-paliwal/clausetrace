# Western Oak University — Code of Student Conduct & Disciplinary Procedures

**Document Reference:** WOU-REG-DSC-2026-V2  
**Effective Date:** August 15, 2026  
**Jurisdiction:** Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards, Division of Student Affairs  
**Applicability:** All Enrolled Students, Registered Student Organizations, and Campus Visitors

---

## Article I: Philosophy, Purpose, and Jurisdictional Authority

Western Oak University is committed to cultivating a safe, ethical, and collaborative scholarly community. The Code of Student Conduct defines expectations for behavioral integrity, civil discourse, and personal responsibility. 

The jurisdiction of this Code extends to student conduct occurring on university premises, at university-sponsored events, off-campus situations that adversely affect the educational mission of the institution, and cyber-conduct impacting campus safety. Every matriculated student affirms compliance with these standards upon completion of semester registration.

---

## Article II: Prohibited Conduct and Behavioral Infractions

### Section 2.1: Academic Misconduct
Academic integrity is the cornerstone of higher education. Violations subject to formal adjudication under this Code include, but are not limited to:
1. **Plagiarism:** Presenting the words, ideas, architectural designs, algorithms, or visual data of another individual without full and explicit scholarly citation.
2. **Unauthorized Collaboration:** Sharing examination answers, laboratory code, homework solutions, or assessment materials without prior instructor permission.
3. **Falsification of Academic Records:** Altering grade reports, forging faculty signatures, tampering with medical exemption certificates, or fabricating scientific laboratory data.
4. **Examination Hall Deception:** Utilizing cheat sheets, concealed cellular devices, unauthorized calculators, or proxy examination test-takers.

### Section 2.2: Non-Academic Behavioral Misconduct
Non-academic violations encompass actions that compromise individual dignity, campus property, or public safety:
1. **Harassment, Stalking, and Intimidation:** Persistent, unwelcome conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, or abusive educational or living environment.
2. **Physical Aggression and Endangerment:** Acts of assault, battery, fighting, reckless endangerment, or threats of physical violence.
3. **Property Destruction and Theft:** Defacement of collegiate buildings, destruction of laboratory assets, theft of peer property, or unauthorized trespass into administrative facilities.
4. **Substance Violations:** Unlawful possession, distribution, consumption, or manufacturing of alcoholic beverages or controlled substances on collegiate grounds.
5. **Disruption of University Operations:** Intentional obstruction of teaching, research, administrative proceedings, campus security operations, or emergency dispatch services.

## Article III: Incident Reporting, Preliminary Inquiry, and Notice

### Section 3.1: Filing an Incident Report
Any member of the university community—faculty, administrative staff, student, or local resident—may file a formal Student Misconduct Report with the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards. 

Reports alleging academic misconduct must be initiated within 15 calendar days of discovery. Reports alleging general behavioral or safety violations must be filed within 30 calendar days of the occurrence, unless involving allegations of sexual violence or serious felony conduct, which have no limitations period.

### Section 3.2: Preliminary Assessment
Upon receiving a report, the Director of Student Conduct conducts a preliminary inquiry within 5 business days to determine:
- Whether the university maintains subject-matter and personal jurisdiction.
- Whether the factual allegations, if substantiated, constitute a violation of this Code.
- Whether the dispute is amenable to alternative conflict resolution or requires formal disciplinary adjudication.

If formal charges are sustained, the Respondent is issued a Written Notice of Allegations via official university email, specifying the specific articles violated, a factual summary of charges, and a date for the Preliminary Administrative Conference.

---

## Article IV: Adjudication Pathways and Hearing Protocols

### Section 4.1: Administrative Resolution Conference
For minor first-time infractions not involving suspension or expulsion, the Respondent may elect an Administrative Resolution Conference with a Hearing Officer. In this setting:
- The Respondent reviews all investigative materials and witness statements.
- The Respondent may admit responsibility and accept mutually agreed sanctioning.
- If no consensus is achieved, the matter is referred directly to the University Student Conduct Board.

### Section 4.2: University Student Conduct Board Hearings
Major behavioral violations and disputed academic dishonesty charges are heard before the University Student Conduct Board, composed of two faculty members, one administrative officer, and two trained student representatives.

**Hearing Procedures:**
1. **Right to Advisor:** Both the Complainant and Respondent may be accompanied by an advisor of their choice, including legal counsel. Advisors may consult privately with their advisee but may not address the board directly.
2. **Evidentiary Standard:** Determinations of responsibility are evaluated strictly under the **Preponderance of the Evidence** standard (more likely than not that the violation occurred).
3. **Presentation of Evidence:** Both parties may submit documented evidence, testify, present relevant witnesses, and pose written questions to opposing witnesses via the Board Chair.
4. **Written Decision:** Within 7 business days following the conclusion of the hearing, the Board issues a formal Written Finding of Responsibility and Sanction Rationale.

## Article V: Disciplinary Sanctions

When a student is found responsible for violating university regulations, one or more sanctions may be imposed, proportionate to the gravity of the infraction and prior conduct records:

1. **Formal Reprimand:** A documented warning recorded in the student's internal conduct file, cautioning that subsequent violations will incur heightened penalties.
2. **Disciplinary Probation:** A formal status imposed for a designated timeframe (typically one or two semesters). Further misconduct during this period mandates immediate suspension.
3. **Restitution:** Financial reimbursement for property damage, laboratory destruction, or unauthorized medical treatment caused by the student's actions.
4. **Educational Requirements:** Mandatory completion of reflective essays, substance abuse education modules, community service, or conflict workshops.
5. **Loss of Privileges:** Revocation of campus residential housing contracts, exclusion from study-abroad programs, or suspension of student employment.
6. **Disciplinary Suspension:** Forced temporary separation from Western Oak University for a specified period (ranging from one semester to two academic years). During suspension, the student is barred from campus grounds.
7. **Permanent Expulsion:** Irrevocable termination of matriculation and permanent barring from university enrollment and premises. Notation is permanently affixed to the student's transcript.

## Article VI: Comprehensive Appeals Framework

### Section 6.1: Disciplinary Hearing Appeals
A student found responsible for a conduct violation may appeal the finding or sanction to the Appellate Review Council. Appeals must be submitted within 10 business days of receiving the written board decision, grounded exclusively on:
- Procedural irregularity that materially affected the outcome.
- Discovery of new, material factual evidence previously unavailable.
- Disproportionate severity of the imposed sanction relative to established precedents.

### Section 6.2: Grade Appeals Adjudication Deadline
In matters where an alleged academic misconduct finding directly influences or dictates an assigned course grade, or where a student initiates a formal challenge against an arbitrary or unjust course grade, the matter enters the unified university grade appeal pathway. 

To ensure complete fairness, evidentiary compilation, and adequate departmental review, the absolute deadline to lodge a formal grade appeal is exactly 45 business days following the official posting of final semester grades. 

All documentation, syllabi comparisons, and written arguments must be submitted to the Student Advocacy & Appellate Hearing Office before the expiration of this 45 business-day period. Submissions made after 45 business days will not be accepted.

---

## Article VII: Conduct Records, Expungement, and Privacy

All student conduct files are maintained by the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards in strict adherence to the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA). Disciplinary files are retained for a minimum period of seven (7) years following the student's graduation or permanent separation. 

Sanctions of Disciplinary Suspension and Expulsion are permanently noted on official university academic transcripts. Students who have received minor sanctions (reprimand or disciplinary probation) may petition for administrative file expungement upon the successful completion of all degree requirements, provided no subsequent conduct infractions occurred during their tenure.
